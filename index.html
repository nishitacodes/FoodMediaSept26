Compiling Flavors

Hi, I'm Nishita. As a Bachelor of Culinary Management student at Taylor's University, my approach to food is deeply passionate, focusing heavily on fine dining plating techniques, precise sauce formulations, butchery, and braising methods.

As a former Computer Science major, to me, a recipe is an algorithm. Just as code requires perfect syntax to execute efficiently, a dish requires immaculate technique, temperature control, and plating precision. My foodie identity isn't just about eating; it's about understanding the architecture of flavor and the logistics of the kitchen pass.

Plating Gastronomy
From Sketch to Service

The Plating Sketchbook

Every plate starts as a diagram. I map out components, placement and garnish long before anything touches the pan, in a notebook, on the plate itself with a marker, or as a clean digital concept.

Notebook Blueprint

The first draft: component lists, garnish notes and a top-down plan of the plate, labelled like a schematic.

Marker on the Plate

Testing spacing on the real plate with whiteboard markers: cucumber, onion, prawn, tomato, capsicum, cilantro and micro greens all mapped out.

Digital Concept

Golden-fried tortilla cones filled with achiote chicken and spherical pineapple pearls, finished with micro-cilantro over an uncooked white rice bed.

Inspiration

Gordon Ramsay

Born in Johnstone, Scotland, Ramsay's journey from athlete to multi-Michelin-starred chef exemplifies rigorous discipline. Beyond his intense TV persona, he is a master technician. I am inspired by his relentless standards, precise butchery, and ability to elevate simple ingredients through foundational cooking methods.
